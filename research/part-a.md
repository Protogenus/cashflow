# Part A — Reference App Research: Node-Based Cash-Flow Planner

*Research compiled 2026-11-03. Every factual claim cites a source URL. Claims that could not be verified are marked UNVERIFIED. Prices verified against multiple review sources but not against the vendors' own pricing pages — treat as current-but-confirm.*

## PocketSmith

**What it does:** A New Zealand-based personal finance app (founded 2008) whose signature capability is calendar-based, long-range cash-flow forecasting: it projects daily account balances weeks to 60 years ahead from scheduled income, bills, and recurring expenses, calling itself "a money time machine." ([moneycheck.com](https://moneycheck.com/pocketsmith-review/), [diversemoney.com](https://diversemoney.com/a-pocketsmith-review-what-you-need-to-know/))

**Core UX approach:** Forecasting is the center of gravity, not a module. Users set up recurring budgets (events on a financial calendar); the app auto-projects a daily predicted balance at the bottom of every calendar day and on a forecast graph. Interaction is calendar-first: toggle what-if scenarios on/off via dots in the sidebar, drill into cashflow/net-worth/income-expense statements. Multi-currency with automatic daily-rate conversion is a headline feature. ([pocketsmith.com](https://www.pocketsmith.com/blog/moving-from-mint-to-pocketsmith-budgets-calendar-and-forecasting/), [jonesandjackson.com](https://www.jonesandjackson.com/2026/07/pocketsmith-review.html))

**Pricing:** Free ($0 — 2 accounts, manual import, 12 budgets, 6-month projection); Foundation $9.99/mo billed annually ($14.95/mo monthly — 6 banks, 10-year forecast); Flourish $16.66/mo annual ($24.95 monthly — 18 banks, 30-year forecast); Fortune $26.66/mo annual ($39.95 monthly — unlimited banks, 60-year forecast). All paid plans carry a 14-day money-back guarantee. Consistent across three independent sources. ([quicken.com](https://www.quicken.com/blog/best-tools-for-tracking-small-business-cash-flow-and-personal-finances/), [redbark.com](https://redbark.com/blog/budgeting-apps-australia-bank-sync), [mymillennialguide.com](https://www.mymillennialguide.com/pocketsmith-review/))

**Strengths:** Deepest consumer long-range forecasting on the market (60-year daily balance projections); multi-currency support; mature what-if tooling; independent/self-funded company; strong human support reputation among long-term users. ([jonesandjackson.com](https://www.jonesandjackson.com/2026/07/pocketsmith-review.html), [pocketkiwi.com](https://www.pocketkiwi.com/post/pocketsmith-review-of-reviews))

**Weaknesses:** Steep learning curve and setup effort (bank feeds, custom rules, categories all need hand-tuning); mobile app is widely described as a watered-down companion — heavy work needs the desktop web app; no direct bill payment; free tier lacks live bank sync; expensive at the top end. ([pocketkiwi.com](https://www.pocketkiwi.com/post/pocketsmith-review-of-reviews), [jonesandjackson.com](https://www.jonesandjackson.com/2026/07/pocketsmith-review.html))

**What users complain about:** Bank-feed sync gaps and miscategorization via its Yodlee aggregator ([askmeoffers.com](https://askmeoffers.com/users-review/pocketsmith-com/)); confusing transfer tracking between accounts; no phone/live-chat support (email only, up to ~2-day replies); "too sophisticated for most people" — paying subscribers admitting they never got accurate output (GIGO) ([askmeoffers.com](https://askmeoffers.com/users-review/pocketsmith-com/)); price questioned against free alternatives. Ratings are polarized: Google Play "PocketSmith Sidekick" 3.5 stars/188 reviews ([play.google.com](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.pocketsmith.app)), Trustpilot ~2.9/5 on a tiny sample ([moneytothemasses.com](https://moneytothemasses.com/quick-savings/tips/the-best-budgeting-apps-in-the-uk-how-to-budget-without-trying)).

**Scenario / what-if / Monte Carlo / node elements:** Has a real what-if system: create named scenarios per account (Settings → Organize accounts & scenarios → Add scenario), attach budgets (income drop, new car payment, etc.) to a scenario via the forecast calendar, then toggle scenarios on/off in the sidebar to see the forecast graph change in real time; multiple scenarios can be combined. ([pocketsmith.com](https://www.pocketsmith.com/plan-ahead/what-if-scenarios/), [pocketsmith.com](https://www.pocketsmith.com/features/budgets-and-planning/)) No Monte Carlo simulation is advertised. Closest visual-graph element of any app in this set: a **Category Sankey widget** — a directional flow graph showing income on the left flowing into category/subcategory expense nodes, with flow width proportional to amount, exportable as PNG. ([learn.pocketsmith.com](https://learn.pocketsmith.com/dashboards/6a6X8SseDBUb5vxQijkBRh/category-sankey-widget/6a6X8SseDDctaNx62vEcz6)) Also notable: PocketSmith ships an **MCP server** so ChatGPT/Claude/AI assistants can connect to its financial data. ([pocketsmith.com](https://www.pocketsmith.com/features/)) No drag-and-drop node canvas.

## Monarch Money

**What it does:** A US/Canada personal-finance app (founded 2019, SOC 2 Type 2) combining spending tracking, budgeting, net-worth and investment monitoring in one polished dashboard, positioned as the premium Mint replacement. ([robberger.com](https://robberger.com/monarch-money-review/), [wealthypot.com](https://wealthypot.com/budgeting-apps/monarch-money/))

**Core UX approach:** A cash-flow philosophy instead of zero-based budgeting: default "Flex budgeting" sorts spending into three buckets (fixed, non-monthly, flex) pre-filled from six months of history; the dashboard surfaces cash flow, upcoming bills, budget progress, net-worth trends and goal progress together. Cash Flow Projection (Core) forecasts account balance several weeks/months ahead from recurring income and bills; a conversational **AI Assistant** answers questions about spending/cash flow/net worth in natural language. ([thepennyhoarder.com](https://www.thepennyhoarder.com/budgeting/monarch-money-review/), [wealthypot.com](https://wealthypot.com/budgeting-apps/monarch-money/))

**Pricing:** Two tiers since April 2026. Monarch Core: $14.99/mo or $99.99/yr. Monarch Plus: $199/yr annual-only (some sources list $199.99 via the App Store). **UNVERIFIED/conflicting:** one source lists Plus at $299/yr for new members — price varies by billing route, confirm on the vendor site. 7-day free trial, no permanent free tier. ([thepennyhoarder.com](https://www.thepennyhoarder.com/budgeting/monarch-money-review/), [beelinger.com](https://beelinger.com/monarch-money-review/), [personalone.org](https://personalone.org/monarch-budgeting-app-review/), [budgetingapps.org](https://budgetingapps.org/apps/monarch))

**Strengths:** Best-in-class UX polish; couples/household sharing on one subscription; strong investment tracking; customizable reports with cash-flow/spending/income sections; very high store ratings (App Store 4.9/~115K ratings, Google Play 4.8/~25.5K reviews). ([budgetingapps.org](https://budgetingapps.org/apps/monarch), [play.google.com](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.monarchmoney.mobile&hl=en_US&gl=US))

**Weaknesses:** No free tier — price is the #1 objection; Core's projection window is short (months); debt payoff (snowball/avalanche) integrates poorly into the budget structure; not a strict envelope system (overspending flagged, not forced); bank connections depend on third-party aggregators (Plaid/Finicity/MX) with variable reliability. ([fincomparelab.com](https://www.fincomparelab.com/reviews/monarch-money-review/), [budgetingapps.org](https://budgetingapps.org/apps/monarch))

**What users complain about:** Bank-sync friction — especially smaller credit unions, Fidelity balance accuracy, and Canadian banks (some users forced to manual entry); transactions posted with wrong sign (+/−) that support blamed on Plaid; miscategorization; slow support (5+ days); no way to save/print a budget snapshot (month-end auto-rewrites budgets to actuals); billing/double-billing incidents. ([trustpilot.com](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.monarchmoney.com), [fincomparelab.com](https://www.fincomparelab.com/reviews/monarch-money-review/), [play.google.com](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.monarchmoney.mobile&hl=en_US&gl=US))

**Scenario / what-if / Monte Carlo / node elements:** Plus-tier **Forecasting** (desktop web only): a net-worth projection built automatically from your actual income/expenses/balances, where you add **life events directly to a timeline** — buying a home, having a kid, retiring, career break — and drag them along the timeline; assumptions adjustable, math updates in real time; multiple scenarios can be modeled. ([monarch.com](https://www.monarch.com/blog/monarch-plus), [thepennyhoarder.com](https://www.thepennyhoarder.com/budgeting/monarch-money-review/)) No Monte Carlo advertised (comparison reviews explicitly point users to Boldin/ProjectionLab for Monte Carlo). ([walletgrower.com](https://walletgrower.com/blog/monarch-money-review-2026)) No node-graph elements.

## Copilot Money

**What it does:** An Apple-first (iOS/iPad/Mac, limited web since Dec 2025; US only, no Android) subscription finance app built around "Copilot Intelligence" AI auto-categorization, rollover category budgets, recurring-bill tracking, investments and net worth in a design-led interface. ([budgetingapps.org](https://budgetingapps.org/apps/copilot), [walletgrower.com](https://walletgrower.com/blog/copilot-money-review-2026))

**Core UX approach:** Automation over assignment: transactions are AI-categorized (improving over time as you correct), budgets adapt to how you spend with rollovers, and a "spending line" gives a daily snapshot of position vs. budget. **Cash flow is retrospective, not forecasting** — charts show income, spending and net income over past periods (MTD/YTD/last 12 months/last 3 months/last 4 weeks). The Oct 2026 **Money Assistant** (iPhone only, still labeled beta in the vendor FAQ) is agentic: it builds a starting budget from your data, answers questions ("Am I on track to retire in 10 years?"), and acts on your behalf after approval. MCP integration also launched Oct 27, 2026. ([aipresso.com](https://aipresso.com/reviews/copilot-money-review), [budgetingapps.org](https://budgetingapps.org/apps/copilot), [appbrain.com](https://www.appbrain.com/appstore/copilot-track-budget-money/ios-1447330651))

**Pricing:** One tier, price depends on billing route. Website/Stripe: $95/yr or $13/mo. App Store in-app purchases: $109/yr (limited-time offer against a $119 list) or $18/mo. One-month free trial, no permanent free tier, plus a demo mode to explore before connecting accounts. ([budgetingapps.org](https://budgetingapps.org/apps/copilot), [walletgrower.com](https://walletgrower.com/blog/copilot-money-review-2026), [forseera.com](https://www.forseera.com/compare/monarch-vs-copilot/))

**Strengths:** Highest design praise in the category (Apple Design Awards finalist, Editors' Choice, Forbes Advisor 5.0 for cash-flow monitoring); genuinely good AI categorization; recurring-charge detection; intra-day investment updates vs. market benchmarks; Zillow-linked real-estate net worth. App Store 4.8/31K ratings. ([budgetingapps.org](https://budgetingapps.org/apps/copilot), [apps.apple.com](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/copilot-track-budget-money/id1447330651))

**Weaknesses:** No forecasting/scenario planning at all — future modeling is the explicit gap vs. Monarch Plus; Apple-only (no Android, limited web missing Goals and Cash Flow tabs); not a zero-based/envelope budgeter; US institutions only; households/multi-login sharing still rolling out. ([lumaledger.app](https://lumaledger.app/blog/copilot-money-vs-monarch-money), [budgetingapps.org](https://budgetingapps.org/apps/copilot))

**What users complain about:** Plaid-layer sync failures (banks disconnected for months); Amex transactions posting on wrong dates with notes copied from other transactions (support blamed Plaid; users disagree the notes bug is Plaid's); billing complaints including double-billing and no phone support; limited export options; deleting an account wipes its balance history; Money Assistant still iPhone-only and beta-labeled despite launch announcement. ([fincomparelab.com](https://www.fincomparelab.com/reviews/copilot-money-review/), [fincomparelab.com](https://www.fincomparelab.com/guides/is-copilot-money-safe/), [apps.apple.com](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/copilot-track-budget-money/id1447330651), [pissedconsumer.com](https://www.pissedconsumer.com/copilot-money/RT-F.html))

**Scenario / what-if / Monte Carlo / node elements:** None. Copilot is the only app here with essentially no forward-looking simulation: no forecasting, no what-if scenarios, no retirement planner, no Monte Carlo, no node/graph visualizations. Its "Money Assistant" answers planning questions ("Am I on track to retire in 10 years?") conversationally but does not expose an interactive scenario model. ([appbrain.com](https://www.appbrain.com/appstore/copilot-track-budget-money/ios-1447330651), [lumaledger.app](https://lumaledger.app/blog/copilot-money-vs-monarch-money))

## Quicken Simplifi

**What it does:** Quicken's modern web/mobile personal-finance app (launched 2020) built around a **Spending Plan** (income minus bills/subscriptions/savings goals = what's safe to spend), 12-month **Projected Cash Flows**, a 15-variable **Retirement Planner**, investment tracking and custom reports. ([quicken.com](https://www.quicken.com/products/simplifi/v3/), [pricelesstay.com](https://pricelesstay.com/blog/simplifi-review))

**Core UX approach:** Forward-looking by default. The Spending Plan is a real-time monthly tally that updates as transactions post and tells you what's safe to spend for the rest of the month. Projected Cash Flows continuously compute upcoming deposits/payments to show balance highs and lows date-by-date up to a year out, auto-updating as real transactions post. The Retirement Planner is a dynamic modeling tool (basic tab: 10 variables; advanced: 15 — age, investments, contributions, target retirement age, life expectancy, retirement expenses/income, returns, pre/post-retirement tax rates) where every change instantly updates the projected retirement balance. ([quicken.com](https://www.quicken.com/blog/best-money-management-apps-for-cash-flow-reporting-in-2026/), [quicken.com](https://www.quicken.com/blog/cost-effective-personal-finance-apps-for-retirement-planning/))

**Pricing:** Single paid plan, no free tier. Standard rate $6.99/mo billed annually ($83.88/yr). First-year promotional rates vary by source: $3.99/mo billed annually, or 50% off at $3.49/mo billed annually — **promotional price UNVERIFIED/conflicting across sources; confirm on quicken.com**. 7-day free trial, 30-day money-back guarantee. ([moneycrashers.com](https://www.moneycrashers.com/simplifi-quicken-review/), [thecollegeinvestor.com](https://thecollegeinvestor.com/33220/simplifi-review/), [quicken.com](https://www.quicken.com/products/simplifi/v3/))

**Strengths:** Only app at this price combining 12-month cash-flow projection with a real retirement planner using live account data; spending plan is widely praised for replacing rigid category budgeting; built-in tax reports (Schedules A & B, Form 1040 line-by-line) — unique in this set; 14,000+ institutions via three aggregators; watchlists for per-merchant spending targets. App Store 4.5/~12K ratings. ([quicken.com](https://www.quicken.com/blog/best-money-management-apps-for-cash-flow-reporting-in-2026/), [pricelesstay.com](https://pricelesstay.com/blog/simplifi-review), [thecollegeinvestor.com](https://thecollegeinvestor.com/33220/simplifi-review/))

**Weaknesses:** First-week confusion for zero-based-budgeting users (event-style model is different); basic investment tracking vs. Monarch/Empower; shallow debt-payoff motivation tooling; doesn't integrate with other Quicken products; price creep complaints ($3.99 → $6.99 standard). ([durofy.com](https://durofy.com/quicken-simplifi-honest-review-90-days-later-im-not-going-back/), [19pine.ai](https://www.19pine.ai/blog/best-money-saving-apps))

**What users complain about:** A **May 2026 production incident (ISS-12384961)** where transactions silently disappeared — users lost historical data, spending plans were corrupted, and Quicken advised against CSV re-import — severely damaged data trust ([community.simplifimoney.com](https://community.simplifimoney.com/bestof/like/p163)); chronic aggregator sync/connectivity issues (reviewers reconciling manually monthly); inconsistent customer support with long resolution times ([quickenvsquickbooks.com](https://www.quickenvsquickbooks.com/)). Positives: "best of Monarch, Copilot and Simplifi" migrations reported on Reddit. ([pricelesstay.com](https://pricelesstay.com/blog/simplifi-review))

**Scenario / what-if / Monte Carlo / node elements:** Quicken markets both "run what-if scenarios" on Projected Cash Flow and scenario modeling in the Retirement Planner ("what if I increase my contribution rate by 2%?") — the interaction model is **variable-adjustment sliders/inputs with instant re-computation**, not branching scenarios. ([quicken.com](https://www.quicken.com/products/simplifi/v3/), [quicken.com](https://www.quicken.com/blog/best-personal-finance-apps-for-millennials-in-2026/)) No Monte Carlo advertised. No node-graph elements.

## Cross-cutting notes: forecasting UX comparison

- **Forecasting is the battleground, but UX models differ.** PocketSmith = calendar of daily balances + toggleable named scenarios. Monarch Plus = timeline with draggable life events + net-worth curve. Simplifi = variable-adjustment retirement model + 12-month balance projection. Copilot = no forecasting at all (retrospective charts only).
- **Scenario UX maturity ranking:** PocketSmith (named, stackable, toggleable scenarios — closest to a "scenario workspace") > Monarch Plus (event-driven timeline modeling) > Simplifi (instant what-if via variable changes, no named branches) > Copilot (none).
- **Node-graph / visual workflow: nobody does it.** The only graph-flow element found in any of the four is PocketSmith's Category Sankey widget (a read-only flow diagram, not an editable canvas). No drag-and-drop node editors, no node-based money-flow modeling anywhere.
- **Monte Carlo: absent everywhere.** None of the four advertise Monte Carlo simulation; comparison reviews explicitly redirect Monte Carlo seekers to dedicated retirement tools (Boldin, ProjectionLab).
- **AI exposure:** Monarch (AI Assistant, Core) and Copilot (Money Assistant, iPhone) use conversational AI for Q&A and setup; PocketSmith and Copilot both ship MCP servers exposing financial data to external AI assistants.
- **Shared failure modes:** All four depend on third-party aggregators (Plaid/Yodlee/Finicity/MX) and all four get hammered for sync failures and misclassified data — data fidelity, not UI, is the top complaint category across the board. Simplifi's May 2026 missing-transactions incident is the most severe trust event.
- **Pricing shape:** PocketSmith is the only one with a real free tier (6-month forecast, manual). Simplifi is the budget option (~$47–84/yr). Copilot is single-tier (~$95–109/yr). Monarch is the most expensive (Core $99.99/yr, Plus $199–299/yr). PocketSmith Fortune is the priciest tier overall ($26.66–39.95/mo).
