## 2. Gap analysis: what nobody does well, and where this app can win

**GAP 1 — Optimization ("find the best plan," not just "show the plan").** None of the surveyed apps search the space of possible allocations for you. What-if is table stakes but it is always *manual*: you propose a change, the tool recomputes. ProjectionLab "scores the plan you typed in and hands you tools to improve it" rather than solving; Boldin's Max Spend and Analog's "solved numbers" are the only solve-direction examples found, and neither works as a brute-force optimizer over short-horizon cash-flow allocations. **This app's brute-force split optimizer (5% steps, top candidates with explanations) has no observed competitor.** ([research/part-b.md](cross-cutting #2); [research/part-c.md](§7 "solved vs. scored spectrum"))

**GAP 2 — Monte Carlo at the short-horizon, bill-level scale.** Monte Carlo in the market means retirement horizons and portfolio returns. Nobody applies probabilistic simulation to *next-90-days checking-account cash flow with variable bills and shock events* — which is exactly where paycheck-to-paycheck users live. ([research/part-a.md](cross-cutting: "Monte Carlo: absent everywhere"))

**GAP 3 — Explainable constraints.** Tools show the low point on a chart; none say *which bills and which date* drove the constraint, or *which node breaks this path*. ProjectionLab's per-trial drill-down ("click a failed trial, see where it went wrong") is the closest observed pattern — and it's read-only. A node-based planner can do live per-node diagnostics per path. ([research/part-c.md](cross-cutting #7))

**GAP 4 — Editable node-graph modeling of money flow.** Only read-only flow diagrams exist (PocketSmith's Sankey, ProjectionLab/Boldin's Sankey). No drag-and-drop node editors anywhere in this market. The n8n/Node-RED interaction grammar is proven; nobody has applied it to personal finance. ([research/part-a.md](cross-cutting); [research/part-d.md](§1))

**GAP 5 — Sub-month resolution as a first-class feature.** Recurna's whole pitch is "on budget for the month but overdraft on a Tuesday" ([recurna.ca](https://www.recurna.ca/)); forecash.io and fore-cash.io build day-level calendars. Incumbents' monthly averages are the recurring complaint. A day-by-day engine with pinch-point detection targets this directly. ([research/part-b.md](cross-cutting #4))

**GAP 6 — Manual-first privacy as a premium position.** Recurna deliberately keeps transaction data off US aggregators ([recurna.ca](https://www.recurna.ca/)); all four mainstream apps get hammered for Plaid/Yodlee sync failures and miscategorization — data fidelity, not UI, is the top complaint category ([research/part-a.md](cross-cutting "Shared failure modes")). A local-only, manual-entry planner sidesteps the #1 complaint vector entirely and turns it into a privacy story.

**GAP 7 — Transparent assumptions.** Every tool's trust landmine is buried assumptions (Boldin's locked free-tier defaults "scare" users into paying; differing scores from identical inputs erode trust). Boldin is the best practice example (published 8.08%/2.54% defaults); Analog's "every number traceable" is the strongest transparency vision. A visible, editable assumptions panel with seeded, reproducible runs is the direct answer. ([research/part-c.md](cross-cutting #5); [research/part-d.md](§4))

**What incumbents do well that we must not regress on:** PocketSmith's toggleable named scenarios (scenario maturity #1) ([pocketsmith.com](https://www.pocketsmith.com/plan-ahead/what-if-scenarios/)); forecash.io's drag-to-reschedule tactile experimentation ([forecash.io](https://forecash.io/)); Recurna's dashed-baseline year-redraw ([recurna.ca](https://www.recurna.ca/)); Boldin's guardrails as the decision-UI model ([help.boldin.com](https://help.boldin.com/en/articles/12571086-new-feature-spending-guardrails-insight)); ProjectionLab's per-trial drill-down ([projectionlab.com](https://projectionlab.com/changelog)).

## 3. UX patterns to adopt and anti-patterns to avoid

### Adopt

**From node-graph tools (n8n, Node-RED, React Flow):**
- Infinite pannable/zoomable canvas, dotted-grid background; floating zoom controls (in/out, fit, 100%, tidy/auto-layout); `screenToFlowPosition()` drop handling; validate connections in `onConnect` — reject invalid ones on drop with a concise reason (cycles, self-loops, duplicate edges, handle type mismatch). (Sources: [n8n canvas docs](https://github.com/scaler-llm-experiments/n8n-judge/blob/HEAD/docs/n8n-reference/01-canvas-and-nodes.md); [react-flow AGENTS.md](https://github.com/thedogwiththedataonit/react-flow/blob/HEAD/AGENTS.md))
- Standard selection grammar: click, shift-click multi-select, marquee, Escape to clear; **both Delete and Backspace** remove selection (Mac has no Delete key); Ctrl+C/V copy/paste, duplicate, Ctrl+A select-all. ([scriptmanager spec](https://github.com/anand-s-kale/scriptmanager/blob/HEAD/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-15-n8n-inspired-workflow-editor-design.md))
- **One undo/redo stack, grouped operations** — drag = one undo step (begin-batch/end-batch), typing debounces ~300–500ms per step. Broken undo "destroys user trust faster than almost any other bug." ([c8c UX remediation plan](https://github.com/bluzir/c8c/blob/HEAD/specs/c8c-workflow-editor-ux-remediation-plan.md))
- Validation at point of edit (inspector field errors), persistent validation count near the compute action, one-click nav from validation list to offending node. ([c8c UX remediation plan](https://github.com/bluzir/c8c/blob/HEAD/specs/c8c-workflow-editor-ux-remediation-plan.md))
- Status overlays on nodes: per-node live values on the card ("next: Nov 15 · $2,140") — our nodes should show computed outputs directly. ([n8n community](https://community.n8n.io/t/ticket-based-cookie-sso-for-n8n-community-edition-complete-design-implementation/278489))
- Auto-layout on demand (dagre), not continuous — explicit "tidy" action preserving freeform positioning. ([react-flow AGENTS.md](https://github.com/thedogwiththedataonit/react-flow/blob/HEAD/AGENTS.md))
- Drag-from-output onto empty canvas → open node picker and auto-wire in one step (n8n's "+" affordance). ([n8n canvas docs](https://github.com/scaler-llm-experiments/n8n-judge/blob/HEAD/docs/n8n-reference/01-canvas-and-nodes.md))

**From forecast / uncertainty visualization:**
- Fan charts for projection uncertainty (Bank of England inflation fan; canonical per FT Visual Vocabulary): use only when bands are real quantiles of a stated model, past-vs-projected boundary is explicit, central line is not "the" forecast, horizon is bounded. ([FT chart-doctor](https://github.com/Financial-Times/chart-doctor/blob/main/visual-vocabulary/README.md); [omniscience chart framework](https://github.com/ideotion/open-omniscience/blob/HEAD/docs/research/dataviz/chart_decision_framework.md))
- Bank of England Working Paper 1,196 (2026): fan charts "perform best overall" for conveying distributional information across expert and household audiences. ([bankofengland.co.uk](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/working-paper/2026/anchors-aweigh-the-effect-of-communicating-forecast-uncertainty))
- **Frequency framing beats abstract intervals**: quantile dotplots / hypothetical outcome plots; label intervals explicitly (95% CI vs SD vs SE). ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-to-get-better-at-embracing-unknowns/?print=true.); [statmodeling.columbia.edu](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2022/03/25/advice-for-the-government-on-communicating-uncertainty/))
- Uncertainty in the main view, not liner notes — what we visualize implies what we think is important. ([statmodeling.columbia.edu](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2022/03/25/advice-for-the-government-on-communicating-uncertainty/))

**From Monte Carlo incumbents:**
- ProjectionLab's per-trial drill-down ("click a failed trial, see where it went wrong") — killer UX pattern to borrow ([projectionlab.com](https://projectionlab.com/changelog)); their public MC explainer framing ("An 85% result does not mean you have a 15% chance of going broke tomorrow") ([projectionlab.com](https://projectionlab.com/financial-terms/monte-carlo-simulation))
- Boldin's Spending Guardrails as the model for turning probability into a decision UI (safe target + warning bands) ([help.boldin.com](https://help.boldin.com/en/articles/12571086-new-feature-spending-guardrails-insight))
- Boldin/Analog: publish default assumptions; Analog's traceability ("open any figure to that year's tax form") as the ideal for our per-node drill-down ([dev.analogcapital.ai](https://dev.analogcapital.ai/why-analog.html))
- Recurna's dashed-baseline comparison when a what-if is toggled ([recurna.ca](https://www.recurna.ca/)); forecash.io's drag-expense-on-graph ([forecash.io](https://forecash.io/))

### Avoid

- **"Probability of success" as the lone headline.** Kitces/Kahneman: clients substitute "how bad would running out of money feel?" for rational evaluation; binary success framing hides outcome spectrum ("died with $3M unspent = success"). Show dollars across scenarios + frequency framing; keep any headline % secondary with the success definition beside it. ([Portfolio Construction Forum](https://obj.portfolioconstructionforum.edu.au/articles_perspectives/PortfolioConstruction-Forum_Do_Our_Brains_Really_Even_Know_How_To_Evaluate_A_Monte_Carlo_Analysis.pdf); [incomelaboratory.com](https://incomelaboratory.com/retirement-income-guardrails-complete-guide/))
- **Black-box verdicts.** The #1 trust erosion vector across tools: buried assumptions, differing scores from identical inputs. Every assumption visible and editable; every number traceable to the node + assumption that produced it. ([research/part-c.md](cross-cutting #5); [research/part-d.md](§4 rule 8))
- **Withholding the probability engine on a free tier / pessimistic defaults as upsell** (Boldin pattern). If this product ever monetizes, the trust engine must be the free, complete piece. ([monteclimb.com](https://www.monteclimb.com/blog/retirement-planning-software-comparison); [thecollegeinvestor.com](https://thecollegeinvestor.com/33680/newretirement-review/))
- **Point forecasts without bands; unrounded false precision.** Lead with the fan/percentile chart; central line = median, labeled as such; round aggressively ($41,800, not $41,832.17). ([research/part-d.md](§4 rules 1, 4))
- **Generic bolted-on disclaimers.** FCA behavioral finding: consumers don't read them. Risk context must be balanced, proportionate, and at the point of the number. ([zenithspark.co.uk](https://zenithspark.co.uk/what-are-the-rules-for-financial-services-copy/); [investmentuk.org](https://www.investmentuk.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/Risk%20Warnings%20Guidance%20final.pdf))
- **Mode confusion** (pan vs. select tools unclear; fix with instant cursor change <100ms), **hidden modifier-key features** (show hints in tooltips), **ungrouped undo**, **feedback gaps** (validation only at compute time). ([windrose skill](https://github.com/alas-poor-ophelia/windrose-md/blob/HEAD/.claude/skills/ui-design/SKILL.md))
- **Canvas treated as a secondary surface.** The graph must be part of the main tab model, editable in place with a side inspector — not a separate "visualization" screen. ([c8c UX remediation plan](https://github.com/bluzir/c8c/blob/HEAD/specs/c8c-workflow-editor-ux-remediation-plan.md))
- **Silent recurrence failures.** `FREQ=MONTHLY;BYMONTHDAY=31` skips short months (RFC 5545: invalid dates "MUST be ignored"). We expose "on the 31st — skips shorter months" vs. "last day of each month" as explicit choices, per DECISIONS.md. ([findutils rrule explainer](https://findutils.com/blog/rrule-explained-how-recurring-calendar-events-work/))
- **Confusing bi-weekly with semi-monthly.** Offer them as distinct options with counts stated ("26 paychecks/yr" vs. "24/yr, on the 1st and 15th") — mis-modeling undercounts a year by ~3.8% of gross. ([storyvergey.com](https://storyvergey.com/d/Dh5JMsNTMxurIzzPMwd6M.pdf); the arithmetic note is UNVERIFIED product judgment)
- **Chaining "+1 month" off the last occurrence.** Anchor every series on an immutable start date; never degrade the 31st→28th clamp down a chain. ([plainspace docs](https://github.com/super-productivity/plainspace/blob/HEAD/docs/plans/repeating-tasks-concept.md))
- **Relegating bank-sync users to a second-class path.** We have no bank sync (see DECISIONS.md), so this anti-pattern can't bite — but CSV import must be honest about what it produces (anchored recurrence suggestions, not certainty).

## 4. Date-logic decisions for the engine (from part-d §3)

These are product decisions derived from the research; the authoritative record is DECISIONS.md.

- **Bi-weekly vs. semi-monthly:** distinct options, counts stated in the UI.
- **Monthly on 31st:** we chose **last-day-of-month clamp** (Jan 31 → Feb 28) as the single documented convention — justified in DECISIONS.md (RFC 5545's default *skips* short months, which means a silently missed bill in a cash-flow planner; clamping matches how most billing systems behave in practice — UNVERIFIED as a universal claim, adopted as the deliberate product convention).
- **Weekend adjustment:** expose Following vs. Preceding per recurring item, defaulting to Preceding for income (paychecks arrive the prior Friday) and Following for expenses (debits move to Monday), per ISDA Business Day Conventions vocabulary. ([isda.org](https://www.isda.org/a/C51EE/FX-Glossary-English.pdf); [veloriftlabs.com](https://veloriftlabs.com/p/rbScTiNpqIag3ucRJqd8b.pdf))
- **Leap years / DST / timezones:** recur on wall-clock local time (not UTC); store occurrences as local wall-clock in the user's zone; Feb 29 series fires only in leap years (stated explicitly); midnight-anchored financial events are DST-insensitive. ([findutils rrule explainer](https://findutils.com/blog/rrule-explained-how-recurring-calendar-events-work/); [medium.com/@findutils](https://medium.com/@findutils/calendar-data-hygiene-filter-scrub-and-expire-recurring-events-in-ics-files-348783ae0d2c))
- **Edited occurrences / exceptions:** one moved/edited occurrence keeps the series rule intact; the exception is stored against the original occurrence (iCalendar `RECURRENCE-ID` pattern). `COUNT` consumes cancelled slots. ([findutils rrule explainer](https://findutils.com/blog/rrule-explained-how-recurring-calendar-events-work/))
